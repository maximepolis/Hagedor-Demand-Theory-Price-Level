WHAT IS SOLVED (and what is not)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Program implemented at announcement w.p. p; once implemented, per-period
reversal hazard h. Probability the program is running at t:
    m_t(p,h) = p*(1-h)^(t-1).

IMPLEMENTED: the CERTAINTY-EQUIVALENT, PV-MATCHED PERMANENT SURROGATE.
    Gg_eff(p,h) = s(p,h)*Gbar,   s(p,h) = p*rbar/(rbar+h),
    s = PV-weighted expected share of dates at which the program runs,
    discounted at the stationary real rate rbar. s(1,0) = 1 exactly.
The solver is then run as a PERFECT-FORESIGHT transition for a permanent
program of size Gg_eff: boundary steady states, terminal pin, climate
block and taxes are all internally consistent with that program.

NOT IMPLEMENTED: the risk is NOT PRICED. A probability-weighted policy
path is NOT the same object as a solution in which the reversal risk is
priced. There is no state-contingent price jump at implementation or at
reversal, no bond risk premium, and no extra precautionary demand coming
from policy uncertainty itself. In a model whose mechanism IS
precautionary asset demand, priced policy risk would raise asset demand
and push the price level DOWN, offsetting the smaller expected program by
an amount this run cannot measure. No net sign is claimed.

SECOND APPROXIMATION: the expected path m_t*Gbar declines when h>0; the
surrogate is the FLAT PV-equivalent path s*Gbar. They coincide exactly at
h=0 (m_t = p = s), so the h=0 column carries ONLY the certainty-equivalence
approximation. Column "flatgap" = max_t |m_t - s| reports the size of the
flattening for h>0. Green capital accumulates, so flattening moves the
TIMING of damages even at matched spending PV.

The hazards h and probabilities p are ILLUSTRATIVE, not estimated. This
driver reports sensitivity, not identification.

SWEEP IS IN THE PV WEIGHT s, NOT THE RAW HAZARD h. At rbar = 0.0196 the map
h -> s = rbar/(rbar+h) is violently convex: h = 0.05 (a 20-year expected
life) already removes 72% of the program PV, which drives the price
response under the solver residual and returns an unresolved grid. There
is no graded middle ground in h at this real rate -- holding s >= 0.9
needs h <= 0.0022, an expected life of 459 years. Implied hazards:
    s = 1.00  ->  h = 0         (permanent)
    s = 0.90  ->  h = 0.00218   (expected life    459 yr)
    s = 0.80  ->  h = 0.00490   (expected life    204 yr)
    s = 0.70  ->  h = 0.00840   (expected life    119 yr)

READING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1 of 16 cells converged but fail the snr>=10 resolution margin:
  their impact response is not separated from the solver's clearing
  residual, so their F_P is not reported. Small s means a small program
  means a small price response; a resolution limit, not a finding.
  linearity ratio across reportable cells: min 0.580, median 0.879, max 1.000.
  If this sits near 1, the impact response is close to PROPORTIONAL to
  s(p,h), and the referee's charge is answered quantitatively: perfect
  credibility scales the announcement response by 1/s relative to a
  program believed with probability p and reversed at hazard h. If it
  departs from 1, the capitalization is nonlinear in program size and
  the scaling argument does not hold.
  F_P across RESOLVED cells: min 0.527, median 0.701, max 0.767.
  Front-loading is a property of the transition dynamics; under this
  PV-matched surrogate a change in (p,h) rescales the program, so a
  NEARLY CONSTANT F_P is the expected outcome and is NOT evidence that
  reversal risk leaves front-loading alone. Establishing that would
  require the declining expected path (approximation 2 removed) and,
  for the real question, the priced-risk solution (approximation 1).

  WHAT THIS DOES NOT SETTLE: the priced-risk economy, the values of p
  and h, and the front-loading of a program that is expected to be
  dismantled rather than merely smaller. None of those is claimed here.
